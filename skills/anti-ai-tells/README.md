# anti-ai-tells

A field guide for stripping the AI-chatbot fingerprint out of prose — puffery, suspicious triplets, "not only X but also Y", trailing -ing analysis, formulaic section structure, em-dash overuse, and the rest of the patterns that signal "this was generated."

The skill body Claude reads is [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md). This file is human-facing context.

## What it covers

Thirteen pattern categories, each with the tell, why it's a tell, and the fix. A twelve-item review checklist for a post-draft pass. Caveats about genre and voice so the skill doesn't flatten into "ban every em-dash."

The categories, in roughly the order readers notice them:

1. Puffery and emphasis on importance ("renowned", "stands as a testament", "plays a pivotal role")
2. The rule of three
3. Negative parallelism ("not only X, but also Y")
4. Editorializing and vague attribution ("some critics argue", trailing -ing clauses)
5. Section-level formulaic structure ("Background / Key Features / Future Prospects")
6. Conclusion phrases ("In summary", "Overall", "Ultimately")
7. Em-dash overuse
8. Anaphora and short-line breaks
9. Excessive Markdown formatting
10. Curly quotes and pasted Markdown artifacts
11. Knowledge-cutoff and meta-disclaimers
12. Sourcing tics (invented citations, vague gestures)
13. The smoothed-out mean — the meta-pattern under most of the above

## When to use it

Two phases, both important:

- **While drafting** — holding the patterns in mind while writing is more effective than catching them after.
- **As a final review pass** — walk the checklist before delivering any long-form prose.

Use it for articles, blog posts, essays, reports, Wikipedia edits, marketing copy, emails, and fiction. Use it as a self-review pass after generating long-form text even if the request didn't say "make this sound human."

Don't use it as a style straightjacket. The skill itself says so: these are tells, not crimes. A careful human writing about a subject they love will sometimes do these things. The signal is combination, density, and predictability — not any single feature.
